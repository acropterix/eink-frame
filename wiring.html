Frame wiring

Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 → Waveshare 13.3" e-Paper HAT+ (E). Follow the pictures; each step has one diagram.

The whole thing

1Panel → adapter → HAT

✅ No voltage switch on this board. The HAT+ (E) is 3.3 V only — there is nothing to set. (The "3.3V/5V" switch you may have read about is on Waveshare's older driver HAT.)

Hold the panel by its edges. Never crease the orange flex where it meets the glass.

2The 10-wire cable: HAT → XIAO

Plug the cable's small white plug into the HAT. Push each black jumper end onto the XIAO pin shown. Hold both boards exactly like this: HAT text readable, XIAO with USB-C at the top and the metal shield facing you.

⚠️ Before plugging in USB: follow each wire from its printed label on the HAT to the XIAO pin — go by the label, not the colour. Red must sit under VCC and go to 3V3; black under GND to GND. Then power up and touch both boards after 10 s — anything hot, unplug.
⚡ Keep the VCC and GND jumpers short and direct (≈10 cm or less), XIAO straight to HAT. The panel draws current in bursts; long or shared power leads make it show only yellow and black.
Test now: USB-C into the Mac, press R on the XIAO. The panel flickers for ~20 s and shows a picture.

3The push button

One button does everything: short press = next picture, hold 3 s = config mode (hold 3 s again, or press Restart on the page, to leave; it also ends by itself after 30 min). No switch, LED or resistor needed.

  1. One button leg → D0 (left side, 1st pin from the top). No resistor: the firmware turns on the pull-up.
  2. Other leg → GND. Leave the HAT's GND jumper where it is and solder the button's wire to the same GND pin from the underside of the XIAO.
  3. Keep the button leads short too, and never route them through the HAT's power wires.

Optional: a toggle switch from D9 to GND still works as a config-mode switch, but you don't need it.

4Battery and battery reading (XIAO underside)

⚠️ Check the battery plug with a multimeter first: red probe on the wire you'll solder to BAT+ must read +3.7 V. Reversed = dead XIAO.

The easy way to solder the tiny pads

  1. Solder a JST-PH pigtail (socket with two short wires) to the pads, never the battery itself. The battery just plugs in afterwards.
  2. Tape the XIAO upside-down to the desk. Add flux, then put a small blob of solder on each pad (≈ 330 °C, chisel or bevel tip). Strip 2 mm of wire, twist, and tin it too.
  3. Tape the wire so its tinned tip lies flat on the pad by itself. Touch the iron on top for 2 s — the two blobs melt together. No third hand needed.
  4. Tug gently, check no bridge between pads, then cover with a dab of hot glue or Kapton so the wires can't flex at the joint.

Battery reading: two resistors, no capacitor (optional)

Lets Home Assistant show the battery %. Skip it and the frame still works; the battery just shows "unknown".

  1. Take two 100 kΩ resistors (220 kΩ also works). Twist one leg of each together and solder: that's the middle. Slide heat-shrink over it.
  2. Do this while soldering the battery pigtail: put the free leg of one resistor into the BAT+ joint with the red wire, and the other into the BAT− joint with the black wire. No extra pads to hit.
  3. Run a short wire from the middle to D3 (left side, 4th pin from the top when the XIAO is face-up). Easiest: cut a female jumper in half, solder it to the middle, push it onto D3.
  4. Hold the button 3 s → http://eink-frame.local → tick Battery divider fitted on D3 → Save.

It draws about 20 µA, roughly 6% of the battery per year. Don't use 1 MΩ resistors without the capacitor: the reading becomes unreliable.
